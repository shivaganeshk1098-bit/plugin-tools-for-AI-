# Draft an email

Write a clear, appropriately-toned email the user can send with minimal edits.

## Steps

1. **Get the essentials.** Who's it to, what's the goal (inform, request, decline,
   apologize, follow up), and what key points must it include? If replying, read
   the original to match context and answer what was asked. Ask only if a critical
   detail is missing.
2. **Pick the tone.** Professional by default; adjust to friendly, formal, or firm
   as the situation needs. Mirror the recipient's level of formality when replying.
3. **Structure it:**
   - **Subject** (for new emails) — short and specific.
   - **Greeting** — appropriate to the relationship.
   - **Body** — lead with the point, then the detail; keep paragraphs short.
   - **Clear ask or next step** — what you want the reader to do.
   - **Sign-off** — matching the tone.
4. **Keep it tight.** Say what's needed and stop. Remove hedging and filler.

## Output

Provide the ready-to-send draft. If tone could go a couple of ways, offer a short
alternative (e.g. a warmer or more formal version).

## Notes

- Don't invent facts, names, dates, or commitments the user didn't provide — leave
  a clearly marked `[placeholder]` instead.
- Be honest and professional even in difficult messages (declines, complaints);
  clarity with courtesy beats vagueness.
