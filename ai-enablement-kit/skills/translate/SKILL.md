# Translate

Translate text so it reads naturally to a native speaker — meaning and tone
intact, not a literal word swap.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the languages.** Identify the source language (detect it if not
   stated) and the target language. If the target isn't given, ask.
2. **Understand before translating.** Grasp the meaning, tone (formal/casual), and
   any context or intent — translate the *message*, not just the words.
3. **Translate naturally:**
   - Preserve tone and register (a formal letter stays formal; slang stays casual).
   - Adapt idioms and cultural references to an equivalent that lands in the target
     language, rather than translating them literally.
   - Keep names, numbers, and formatting intact.
4. **Flag genuine ambiguity.** If a word or phrase has multiple valid renderings or
   doesn't map cleanly, give the best choice and briefly note the alternative.

## Output

- The **translation**, ready to use.
- **(If asked or helpful)** a romanization/pronunciation guide, or a note on
  formality choices (e.g. formal vs. informal "you").

## Notes

- Prioritize how a native speaker would actually say it over literal fidelity.
- For short phrases, you can offer both a formal and a casual version.
- Say so honestly if a language or dialect is outside your confidence rather than
  producing an unreliable translation.
